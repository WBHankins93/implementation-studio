# Account Strategy

## 📝 Context

You've moved past initial POC. Now you're thinking strategically about this account - 
how to expand from one division to others, displace competitors, build executive 
relationships, and turn this into a reference customer. This is the strategic layer 
above tactical execution.

## 📋 Account Planning Checklist

- [ ] Understand account structure (divisions, regions, business units)
- [ ] Map decision makers and influencers
- [ ] Identify current vendor landscape
- [ ] Understand business priorities and initiatives
- [ ] Assess competitive positioning
- [ ] Identify expansion opportunities
- [ ] Understand budget cycles and planning
- [ ] Build executive relationships

## 🎯 Account Mapping

**Organizational Structure**

- What divisions/business units exist?
- How centralized vs. decentralized is IT?
- Who controls budget at each level?
- How do divisions interact/influence each other?
- Are there shared services organizations?

**Stakeholder Mapping**

Create a stakeholder map:

| Name | Title | Division | Role | Influence | Relationship | Champion? |
|------|-------|----------|------|-----------|--------------|-----------|
| [Name] | VP Engineering | Corp IT | Decision maker | High | Strong | Yes |
| [Name] | Director | Division A | Technical evaluator | Medium | Building | Potential |
| [Name] | Architect | Division B | Influencer | High | New | No |

**Relationship Strength:**
- 🟢 Champion - Actively selling internally for you
- 🟡 Supporter - Positive but not advocating
- ⚪ Neutral - No opinion or unknown
- 🔴 Blocker - Opposing or skeptical

**Decision Process:**

- Who approves purchases at different levels?
- What's the approval process?
- Who has veto power?
- What's the timeline for decisions?
- Are there committee reviews?

## 🎯 Competitive Landscape

**Current Vendor Relationships**

- Who are you displacing or competing with?
- What's the incumbent relationship strength?
- What's the switching cost?
- What's their contract/renewal timeline?
- Why are they looking at alternatives?

**Competitive Positioning**

- What do you do better than incumbent?
- What do they do better than you?
- What's your differentiation message?
- What are their pain points with current vendor?
- How do you position against alternatives?

**Displacement Strategy**

- Land and expand (start small, prove value, grow)
- Competitive displacement (directly replace incumbent)
- Net new use case (solve something they can't today)
- Hybrid approach

## 🎯 Expansion Strategy

**From POC to Production**

- POC success → Production deployment
- Production → Day-2 operations and support
- Day-2 → Optimization and expansion
- Expansion → Other teams/divisions

**Horizontal Expansion**

- POC in Division A → Division B, C, D
- Use case 1 → Use case 2, 3, 4
- Team 1 → Team 2, 3, 4
- Leverage early wins to accelerate

**Vertical Expansion**

- Individual contributor → Manager
- Manager → Director
- Director → VP/Executive
- Build relationships at each level

**Expansion Triggers**

- POC success → Production deployment
- Production success → Expansion to other teams
- Executive sponsor → Other divisions
- Reference customer → Broader adoption
- Annual planning cycle → Budget allocation

## 🎯 Executive Relationship Building

**Why Executive Relationships Matter:**

- Accelerate decisions
- Navigate politics
- Access budget
- Strategic alignment
- Reference customer cultivation
- Expansion opportunities

**How to Build Executive Relationships:**

1. **Earn the introduction**
   - Don't ask to meet executives without reason
   - Build credibility at working level first
   - Bring value to the conversation

2. **Executive engagement scenarios**
   - POC kickoff or results presentation
   - Strategic planning discussions
   - Escalation resolution (done well)
   - Industry insights or competitive intelligence
   - Executive briefing or roadmap review

3. **Executive communication style**
   - Business outcomes, not technical features
   - Strategic alignment with their initiatives
   - Risk mitigation and competitive positioning
   - ROI and value realization
   - Brief and focused (they have limited time)

4. **Maintain the relationship**
   - Quarterly check-ins (don't disappear)
   - Share industry insights
   - Invite to relevant events
   - Provide value beyond your product
   - Build trust over time

**Example Executive Messaging:**

Bad: "Our platform has 15 features that..."

Good: "Based on our work with your team, we've validated that you can reduce deployment time by 60% and operational costs by 40%, which aligns with your initiative to modernize infrastructure while controlling budget."

## 🎯 Reference Customer Cultivation

**Why Reference Customers Matter:**

- Accelerate sales cycles
- Provide social proof
- Competitive differentiation
- Marketing and PR value
- Influence product roadmap

**How to Identify Reference Potential:**

- Successful implementation (working well)
- Brand name recognition (known company)
- Willing to share their story
- Represents key vertical or use case
- Executive sponsorship (will speak publicly)

**How to Cultivate Reference Customers:**

1. **Deliver exceptional outcomes**
   - POC that exceeds expectations
   - Smooth implementation
   - Ongoing support and success
   - Solve real business problems

2. **Build relationship capital**
   - Understand their goals and help achieve them
   - Be responsive and reliable
   - Go above and beyond when possible
   - Build trust over time

3. **Make the ask strategically**
   - After clear success (don't ask too early)
   - Explain the value to them (visibility, influence)
   - Make it easy (we do the work)
   - Offer something in return (roadmap influence, priority support)

4. **Types of reference activities**
   - Case study or success story
   - Reference call with prospects
   - Conference speaking or panel
   - Joint webinar or event
   - Quote or testimonial
   - Site visit for prospects

**Example Pitch:**

"You've had great success with [solution], seeing [specific outcomes]. We'd love to share your story as a case study. This would give you visibility in the [industry] and position [Company] as a leader in [area]. We'll handle all the work - just need your approval and a brief interview. In return, we'll prioritize your feedback on our roadmap and provide [benefit]."

## 🎯 Annual Planning Cycles

**Why Planning Cycles Matter:**

- Budget allocation happens annually (or quarterly)
- Strategic initiatives get funded
- Multi-year commitments are made
- Vendor consolidation decisions happen
- Expansion opportunities are approved

**Key Planning Cycle Activities:**

**Q4 (Planning for next year):**
- Understand budget planning timeline
- Influence strategic initiatives
- Get on the budget for next year
- Position for annual renewal or expansion
- Align with their priorities

**Q1 (Budget approved):**
- Execute on approved budget
- Start approved initiatives
- Baseline for the year
- POCs and evaluations for future quarters

**Mid-year:**
- Progress reviews
- Course corrections
- Additional budget requests
- Expansion opportunities

**How to Position for Planning Cycles:**

1. **Understand their planning process**
   - When does planning happen?
   - Who's involved in planning?
   - What's the approval process?
   - What initiatives are priorities?

2. **Align with their goals**
   - Map your solution to their initiatives
   - Show ROI and business case
   - Position for budget allocation
   - Demonstrate value before planning cycle

3. **Get included in the budget**
   - Early conversations (Q3 for next year)
   - Build business case with them
   - Provide budget estimates
   - Support their internal approval process

## ⚠️ Gotchas

- Focusing only on tactical execution - miss strategic opportunities
- Not mapping stakeholders - blindsided by politics
- Ignoring competitive landscape - displaced by incumbent
- Not building executive relationships - stuck at working level
- Treating every account the same - strategy must be contextual
- Over-promising on expansion - deliver on current scope first
- Not understanding budget cycles - miss planning windows
