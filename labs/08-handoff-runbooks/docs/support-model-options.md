# Support Model Options

**Application:** [Application Name]  
**Purpose:** Define support transition options

## Overview

This document outlines different support model options for transitioning from implementation to ongoing operations. Choose the model that best fits your customer's needs and capabilities.

## Support Model Options

### Model 1: Immediate Handoff with Support Window

**Best For:** Customer teams with strong technical capabilities

**Structure:**
- **Immediate Handoff:** Full operational responsibility transferred immediately
- **Support Window:** 30-90 days of enhanced support
- **Ongoing Support:** Standard support model after window

**Support Levels:**

**First 30 Days:**
- **Availability:** Business hours + on-call
- **Response Time:** < 2 hours
- **Support Channels:** Email, Slack, Phone
- **Focus:** Immediate issues, confidence building

**Days 31-90:**
- **Availability:** Business hours + on-call
- **Response Time:** < 4 hours
- **Support Channels:** Email, Slack
- **Focus:** Complex issues, knowledge transfer

**Ongoing:**
- **Availability:** Business hours
- **Response Time:** < 24 hours
- **Support Channels:** Email, Ticket system
- **Focus:** Standard support

**Pros:**
- Customer feels empowered immediately
- Clear transition timeline
- Gradual support reduction
- Customer builds confidence

**Cons:**
- Requires strong customer team
- May need more initial support
- Customer must be ready

### Model 2: Gradual Transition

**Best For:** Customer teams needing more time to build confidence

**Structure:**
- **Phase 1 (Weeks 1-2):** Implementation team leads, customer observes
- **Phase 2 (Weeks 3-4):** Customer leads, implementation team supports
- **Phase 3 (Weeks 5-6):** Customer operates independently, implementation team available
- **Phase 4 (Week 7+):** Full handoff, standard support

**Support Levels:**

**Phase 1:**
- **Availability:** Full-time support
- **Response Time:** Immediate
- **Support Channels:** All channels
- **Focus:** Knowledge transfer, confidence building

**Phase 2:**
- **Availability:** Business hours + on-call
- **Response Time:** < 1 hour
- **Support Channels:** Email, Slack, Phone
- **Focus:** Guided operations, problem-solving

**Phase 3:**
- **Availability:** Business hours + on-call
- **Response Time:** < 2 hours
- **Support Channels:** Email, Slack
- **Focus:** Independent operations, escalation support

**Phase 4:**
- **Availability:** Business hours
- **Response Time:** < 24 hours
- **Support Channels:** Email, Ticket system
- **Focus:** Standard support

**Pros:**
- Gradual confidence building
- More time for knowledge transfer
- Lower risk
- Customer feels supported

**Cons:**
- Longer transition period
- More resource intensive
- May delay full empowerment

### Model 3: Co-Management

**Best For:** Complex systems, shared responsibility

**Structure:**
- **Shared Operations:** Implementation and customer teams share responsibility
- **Defined Roles:** Clear division of responsibilities
- **Ongoing Partnership:** Long-term collaboration

**Support Levels:**

**Operations:**
- **Implementation Team:** Infrastructure, monitoring, complex issues
- **Customer Team:** Day-to-day operations, application management
- **Shared:** Incident response, upgrades, major changes

**Support:**
- **Availability:** Business hours + on-call
- **Response Time:** < 2 hours
- **Support Channels:** Email, Slack, Phone
- **Focus:** Collaborative operations

**Pros:**
- Leverages both teams' strengths
- Lower customer burden
- Faster issue resolution
- Strong partnership

**Cons:**
- Requires ongoing coordination
- May delay customer independence
- Higher cost
- Dependency on implementation team

### Model 4: Managed Service

**Best For:** Customer teams with limited technical resources

**Structure:**
- **Full Management:** Implementation team manages operations
- **Customer Interface:** Customer team provides requirements and feedback
- **Ongoing Service:** Long-term managed service

**Support Levels:**

**Operations:**
- **Implementation Team:** Full operational responsibility
- **Customer Team:** Requirements, feedback, business decisions

**Support:**
- **Availability:** Business hours + on-call
- **Response Time:** < 1 hour
- **Support Channels:** All channels
- **Focus:** Full service management

**Pros:**
- Customer focuses on business
- Professional operations
- Fast issue resolution
- No customer burden

**Cons:**
- Customer dependency
- Higher cost
- Less customer learning
- Less customer empowerment

## Support Model Selection

### Decision Factors

**Customer Team Capability:**
- Strong technical team → Model 1 or 2
- Moderate technical team → Model 2
- Limited technical team → Model 3 or 4

**System Complexity:**
- Simple system → Model 1
- Moderate complexity → Model 2
- Complex system → Model 3 or 4

**Customer Preferences:**
- Want independence → Model 1 or 2
- Want partnership → Model 3
- Want full service → Model 4

**Budget:**
- Limited budget → Model 1
- Moderate budget → Model 2
- Higher budget → Model 3 or 4

## Support Transition Plan

### Phase 1: Enhanced Support (First 30 Days)

**Goals:**
- Build customer confidence
- Address immediate issues
- Transfer knowledge
- Establish processes

**Activities:**
- Daily check-ins
- Immediate issue response
- Knowledge transfer sessions
- Process establishment

### Phase 2: Standard Support (Days 31-90)

**Goals:**
- Customer independence
- Reduced support dependency
- Process refinement
- Documentation updates

**Activities:**
- Weekly check-ins
- Standard issue response
- Process refinement
- Documentation updates

### Phase 3: Ongoing Support (Day 91+)

**Goals:**
- Customer self-sufficiency
- Standard support model
- Continuous improvement
- Long-term partnership

**Activities:**
- Monthly check-ins
- Standard support
- Continuous improvement
- Partnership maintenance
